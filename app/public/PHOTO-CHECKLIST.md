# Fenster Job Photo Checklist

Date started: 2026-07-16
Why: the website's biggest content gap is real photos of our own installs. The image audit (see `ACTION-PLAN.md` and `PROGRESS.md`) removed stock photos, CGI renders and wrong-product images; supplier photography is filling the gap short term, but photos of Fenster jobs are the only durable fix. Five phone photos per job is enough.

## The 5-shot checklist (per completed job)

1. **Straight-on exterior** — stand square to the wall, whole product in frame, no vans/tools/bins in shot.
2. **Interior view** — the product from inside the room, curtains/blinds open, lights off if bright.
3. **Detail close-up** — handle, lock, meeting rail, threshold or glazing bar. One arm's length away.
4. **Open position** — sash lifted, casement open, bifold folded back, slider open.
5. **Street/context shot** — the product on the house from the pavement or drive (this powers the town pages).

## Rules that make photos usable

- Landscape orientation, phone held level.
- Daylight, not dusk. Avoid shooting into the sun; put the sun behind or beside you.
- Wipe the glass and remove protective tape/labels first.
- No people, faces, cars with readable plates, or house numbers close-up (blur-worthy details cost editing time).
- If the customer is around, ask: "Do you mind if we take a few photos of the finished work for our website?" Verbal yes is fine; note the first name and town.

## Special brief: the misted-unit before/after pair (2026-08-10)

For `/double-glazing-replacement/`. The page carries a drag-across slider that
wipes between the misted unit and the new one, so **the two photographs have to
be the same window from the same spot.** Everything else on this list is a nice
shot; this one only works if the camera does not move.

**The one rule: take the "before" shot before anyone touches the window, and
mark where you are standing.** A paving slab, a chalk cross, a bit of tape on
the drive. Stand on that same mark for the "after". Same phone, same zoom (do
not pinch-zoom at all — walk closer or further away instead), same height.

1. **Before.** Outside, square to the window, whole sash in frame with a bit of
   frame around it. Landscape.
2. **After.** Same mark, same framing, once the new unit is in and the glass is
   cleaned. No stickers, no tape, no suction cups.

**Misting is genuinely hard to photograph, so:**

- **Overcast is better than sunny.** Direct sun puts reflections on the glass
  that hide the misting completely. Shade works. Early or late is fine.
- **Never shoot into the sun**, and watch for your own reflection.
- Misting shows best **against a dark interior**, so if the room behind is dark,
  that is a good thing, not a problem. If there is a blind, leaving it down can
  actually make the haze read better.
- Take five or six of each rather than one. The difference between a photo where
  the misting is obvious and one where it vanishes is small and hard to judge on
  a phone screen in daylight.

**Also worth having from the same job, in rough priority order:**

3. **The bead coming out** — this is the shot that proves the frame is untouched,
   and there is nothing like it anywhere in the image bank.
4. **The old unit out of the frame**, ideally stood up where the failed edge seal
   is visible.
5. **The new unit going in.** Hands and tools in shot are good here; this is the
   one section of the page that is about the work rather than the result.
6. **A wide exterior of the elevation** with the misted unit among clear ones,
   landscape and from far enough back to read as a house. That is the page hero
   and there is currently no honest candidate for it.

**Integral blinds, separately and just as wanted:** a unit going into a frame we
did not fit, before and after if possible, plus one with the blind halfway. The
owner has confirmed this is a real and frequent job and the page gives it a
section, but the image bank has nothing showing it retro-fitted.

## Naming and handover

- Send the same day to the office (WhatsApp group or email to info@) with: **product type, colour/finish, town** (e.g. "Flush casement, Anthracite, Bletchley").
- Office saves to the shared job-photos folder under `YYYY-MM product town/`.
- The website pass picks the best shots quarterly; a good street shot from each of the 13 matrix towns is a standing wishlist.
